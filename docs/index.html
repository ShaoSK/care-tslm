TimeNet Hackathon · Round 1 · Team Samosa

Can a time-series language model warn of a wind-turbine fault before it happens?

We fine-tuned OpenTSLM (Flamingo + Llama-3.2-1B) to look at a turbine's last 30 days of SCADA curves every hour, say whether a fault is building up and explain why — then tested it the hard way: official CARE-Score, every turbine held out in turn, protocol fixed before the run.

Short version: on turbines it had not seen, the model did not beat a simple baseline. This page shows the result as measured, what we learned from it, and a follow-up that acts on those lessons.

CARE-Score rewards caution: on our development turbine a model that raised just 3 hourly alarms scored 0.57, because perfect accuracy on normal events props the score up (a model that never alarms at all scores 0). Case study: the main-bearing fault B77

This is the one event where the follow-up model (v4, described below) clearly worked — on a turbine it never trained on. Offshore turbine B-12 was stopped for rotor (main) bearing damage. CARE scores the 63 days before that; its labelled anomaly window (shaded) covers the last 60. The time axis counts days to the end of the window.

1

The two main-bearing sensors stop agreeing

Each sensor is turned into a residual: how far it reads from what this turbine normally reads under the same weather and load. Two sensors on one bearing normally move together, so the difference of their residuals (the line) sits near 0 whatever the season or load. Here it has already moved far below its healthy level when the scored period begins, and stays there.

Rotor bearing temperature 1 minus rotor bearing temperature 2, in units of the difference's healthy spread (0.29 σ on this turbine), clipped at ±8. The dashed lines mark the healthy level and ±3 spreads. Gaps are hours when the turbine was not producing power.

2

The model rates almost every hour above this turbine's own threshold

P(anomaly) is read from the model's likelihood of the two possible answers. The threshold is the 95th percentile of the model's scores on this turbine's healthy training period — set without any label, so a turbine the model sees as "always a bit odd" is calibrated away. Inside the window, 88% of the hours are above it.

3

CARE counts it as detected; the pre-registered model never alarmed

Criticality rises by one for every 10 minutes in alarm and falls by one otherwise; above 72 the event counts as detected. The residual baseline also detects B77 — but it falsely alarms on this turbine's normal event B86 too, which v4 does not.

4

And it says why — in the right place

A generated answer from inside the window. It names the right component (the operator's record says rotor bearing) and the right evidence (the two bearing sensors no longer track each other). All 61 daily answers inside the window say anomaly.

Read the words, not the numbers. The answers are close to a template, and the numbers in them are wrong: the model writes a healthy spread of 0.18 σ and "49.79 °C normally"; the true values for this turbine are 0.29 σ and 48.24 °C, and the model is never shown absolute readings at all. It reads the direction of a change well and quoted numbers badly (in our measurement, off by about 21 percentage points on average).

What this case does not show: early warning from nothing. The bearings had already diverged when the scored period started; the model picks that up from the first hours and holds it until the fault.

Where it did not work

The follow-up (v4) was designed after seeing the pre-registered result, so it does not replace it. It changed training, not evaluation: anomaly examples only from the 7 events with visible evidence; sensor-pair differences as extra input curves; a random constant added to each residual during training (so "this turbine runs warm" stops being informative); and the per-turbine threshold above. It was run on the four turbines whose events include one with visible evidence; folds and the main threshold were fixed before the run.

What we learned

01

A model can only learn faults that leave a trace

Two independent studies of all 18 anomaly events found visible evidence in our channels for 7 (farm A 2/12, farm B 5/6). All six hydraulic faults are invisible in 10-minute temperature means. Fine-tuning worked where the signal was strong (B77) and nowhere else.

02

The explanation is part of the training signal

On the development turbine, a model trained to output only "anomaly / normal" could not rank hours (AUC 0.42); the same model trained to write the evidence too reached 0.75. Writing out what to look at, compared with what, seems to teach the model where to look. (One turbine; not yet confirmed on held-out folds.)

03

Two sensors beat one

The difference between two sensors of one component (main bearing 1 vs 2, transformer core vs cell) cancels season, load and model bias. Four of the seven findable events are only findable this way — B77 among them.

04

Labels confounded with season

In farm A, anomaly snapshots are 5–7 °C warmer on average and cluster in summer and autumn — a shortcut a model can learn without seeing any fault.

05

Greedy decoding is a hidden threshold

Trained on ~80% normal data, the model rarely puts more than half its mass on "anomaly". Reading probabilities and calibrating each turbine on its own healthy period made it speak up; the order of the answer fields mattered too.

06

One fold is not evidence

Retraining the same configuration on the same fold moved CARE-Score by 0.10 (GPU non-determinism), and our best development score (0.777) became 0.000 on the next turbine.

What we would do next

Detector + TSLM, together

The baseline is sensitive but noisy (5 of 5 normal events falsely alarmed on the follow-up folds); v4 is quieter. As a first check, alarming only when both agree kept the B77 detection and cut false alarms to 1 of 5 (CARE 0.512, accuracy 0.94). That rule was chosen after seeing the results, so it is a lead, not a result. Next: let a classical detector trigger and the TSLM verify and explain — or feed the detector's score to the TSLM as one more curve.

More expert knowledge in the labels

Restricting anomaly examples to events with evidence was itself expert input. Turbine engineers could mark when a precursor starts and which signal shows it, and name the sensor pairs that should track each other — the strongest feature we have.

Richer explanation chains

If the explanation is what teaches the model where to look, better targets should help: per-component reasoning steps, comparisons against the turbine's own history, and explicit "no evidence" answers for faults the data cannot show.

Signals the faults actually leave

Hydraulic faults show up in pressure, oil level and pump cycling, not in 10-minute temperature means. Farm C (58 more events, 238 sensors) and the min / max / std channels are the next data to add.

How it works

CARE to Compare SCADA (10-min)→ same-condition residuals per sensor→ hourly snapshot: 30 d @ 4 h + 7.5 d @ 1 h→ OpenTSLM-Flamingo, Llama-3.2-1B→ Evidence · Component · Answer→ 24 h persistence → CARE-Score

Each component sensor is given as a residual: how far it reads from what this turbine normally reads at the same ambient temperature, load and wind, fitted on the first half of its training year (which never becomes a sample). The model starts from the released OpenTSLM ECG-QA-CoT checkpoint and is trained as a new curriculum stage. Training targets are generated from the data; the component is the operator's root-cause label. Folds are by turbine; every threshold in the target text comes from training turbines only.

Data: CARE to Compare (Fraunhofer IEE, CC-BY-SA-4.0), farms A and B, 37 events, 31,842 hourly snapshots. We contributed a TimeNet connector for the full release: OpenTSLM/TimeNet#792.

Limitations

One training run per fold. Farms A and B only. Explanations are generated text with unreliable numbers. The follow-up is post hoc, covers four folds and changes three things at once, so neither its gain nor the effect of each change is established. The training pipeline reads the release through the connector's CSV parser; reading from a built TimeNet dataset is implemented but was not verified end to end.
